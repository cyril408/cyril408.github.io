Since the Council reached its general approach on 9 November 2026 on removing the €150 cap on the Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) and the deemed supplier regime, the summaries have multiplied. Most get the headline right: no threshold, one IOSS registration, application from 1 July 2028 at the earliest.

Two shortcuts keep coming back, including in specialist tax news. Both matter in practice. For the full analysis of the reform and of the new customs status of importer for distance sales, see [my article of 13 November]({{ site.baseurl }}/blog/2026/11/13/ioss-150-threshold-removal-deemed-supplier-importer-for-distance-sales/).

## 1. "Non-EU platforms" will become liable: no, any platform

The common line is that non-EU online platforms will collect VAT on all imported goods, whatever their value. The Council text (document 15480/26) does not say that.

Article 14a of the VAT Directive has two paragraphs with different scopes:

- **Article 14a(1)** covers distance sales of goods imported from third countries. It applies to the platform facilitating the sale, **wherever the platform is established**. This is the paragraph whose €150 limit is being deleted.
- **Article 14a(2)** covers supplies of goods already in the EU. It applies only where the **underlying seller** is established outside the EU. The non-EU test sits here, and it concerns the seller, not the platform.

In practice, a French, German or Dutch marketplace hosting third-country sellers who ship from outside the EU will be the deemed supplier for those sales from 2028, including for a €2,000 laptop. An EU platform that reads the headlines as "this is about the big Asian marketplaces" is likely to underestimate its own exposure.

## 2. Goods "imported in bulk" into customs warehouses: no, under suspension

Some briefs describe the extension of the IOSS to customs warehouses as allowing goods to be "imported in bulk" and then sold individually. Technically, it works the other way round.

Goods in a customs warehouse are under a **suspensive procedure**. They are not imported for VAT purposes. Under Article 61 of the VAT Directive, importation takes place when the goods leave the procedure. That is precisely why the reform can treat a sale shipped from such a warehouse as a distance sale of imported goods and run it through the IOSS, parcel by parcel.

Scope matters too. Only the new **customs warehouse for distance sales** created by Article 145 of the new Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) 2026/2108) is covered. Ordinary customs warehouses stay under the normal VAT rules (recital 4 of the Council text). And that new warehouse is reserved for IOSS users holding Trust and Check status, or their indirect representatives holding it. The authorisation chosen will decide the VAT treatment of the whole stock model.

## Why it matters

The real change in this reform is not below €150, where VAT has applied since July 2021 and the IOSS already exists. It is **everything above €150**: higher stakes per transaction for platforms, B2B/B2C identification, returns on high-value goods, and the alignment of the VAT and customs roles in each flow.
