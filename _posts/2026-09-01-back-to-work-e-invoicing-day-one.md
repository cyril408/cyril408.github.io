There is a particular kind of jet lag that comes from returning to a full inbox after a proper break, and I'll admit to feeling it this morning. But today isn't just another back-to-work Tuesday: 1 September 2026 is also the date the French e-invoicing reform actually starts biting.

## What actually changes today

As of today, every VAT-liable business established in France must be able to **receive** electronic invoices. Large companies and ETIs (mid-size enterprises) must also start **issuing** them electronically. SMEs and micro-businesses get a further year: their own issuance obligation only starts on 1 September 2027, though they too must be able to receive from today.

The detail worth flagging to anyone who hasn't looked at this closely since the plans shifted: the **Portail Public de Facturation (PPF)**, once meant to double as a free, universal invoicing platform, no longer plays that role. Since October 2024, it has been scaled back to two functions only — a central directory of businesses and their e-invoicing addresses, and a data concentrator that relays fiscal information to the DGFiP. Actually issuing and receiving invoices now has to go through a **PDP** (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire), a private platform registered and approved by the DGFiP. If your business doesn't have one lined up, that's the practical gap to close first — not a portal to wait for.

## Why the first day matters more than the deadline

Reforms like this tend to get attention only when the compliance deadline is imminent. But the businesses that treat 1 September as the day the clock starts, rather than the day it runs out, are the ones that end up choosing their PDP deliberately instead of under pressure — and that actually test their invoice data quality before it's flowing to the tax authority in real time.

If you're a large company or ETI, this is no longer a planning exercise; it's live today. If you're an SME with a year of runway, that year goes faster than it looks — the businesses I've seen handle this well started evaluating PDPs well before their own deadline was close.

Good to be back. More soon.
