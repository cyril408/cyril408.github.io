On 9 November 2026, EU Member States reached a general approach in the Council on the directive removing the €150 threshold for the VAT Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) and the deemed supplier regime. The target date is 1 July 2028, provided the EU Customs Data Hub is operational.

This text closes the loop opened by the new Union Customs Code, Regulation (EU) 2026/2108, adopted on 16 September 2026, which creates the "importer for distance sales". From 2028, a seller or a platform could carry both the VAT debt and the customs debt on every distance sale of imported goods, whatever their value.

Two caveats from the outset. The customs side is already law; the VAT side is not yet. And the two statuses do not overlap automatically: that is where the real work lies.

## What the VAT text changes

The text creates no new tax. Since 1 July 2021, VAT has been due on every import, with no exemption. What changes is how it is collected above €150. The text stems from proposal COM(2023) 262 (procedure 2023/0158(CNS)), tabled in May 2023 as part of the customs reform package.

Three measures, according to the Council's general approach (document 15480/26):

1. **No more threshold.** The deemed supplier regime (Article 14a(1) of the VAT Directive) and the IOSS will cover all distance sales of imported goods. Excise goods remain excluded from the IOSS (Article 369l).
2. **Customs warehouses for distance sales.** The definition of distance sales of imported goods (Article 14(4), point 2) is extended to goods dispatched from the new customs warehouses for distance sales created by Article 145 of the new code. Only this type of warehouse is covered: for other customs warehouses, the normal VAT rules continue to apply (recital 4).
3. **Safeguards on intermediaries.** For sellers established in a third country with no mutual assistance agreement, the Member State of identification will check the administrative capacity, financial solvency and probity of their IOSS intermediary. The criteria will be set by the Council, acting unanimously under Article 397, on a Commission proposal due by 31 December 2027 (new Article 369o(2)). The Commission must also report on the scheme by 1 December 2030.

One point worth noting: the Commission's press release refers to an implementing act for the intermediary criteria. The Council text says the Council itself will set them, unanimously. A press release is not the law.

**Procedure.** This is a tax directive under Article 113 TFEU: unanimity in the Council, with the European Parliament only consulted. Parliament gave a first opinion (603 in favour, 23 against, 10 abstentions) and, at the Council's request, will be consulted again before formal adoption. The text can still change, and unanimity has yet to be secured.

## The customs side: a status already adopted

The customs reform is no longer "in progress". Regulation (EU) 2026/2108 was adopted on 16 September 2026, published in the Official Journal on 19 September and repeals Regulation (EU) No 952/2013. What remains ahead are its application dates and delegated acts.

The e-commerce core of the new code:

- **Importer for distance sales** (Article 5, point 14): either the person supplying the goods or the person facilitating the distance sale. "Distance sales" borrow the VAT definition of Article 14(4), point 2, of the VAT Directive (Article 5, point 15).
- **Designation by arrangement.** The importer is the supplier or the facilitator "depending on the arrangements made", and must be indicated as such (recital 63).
- **Non-EU operators.** An indirect representative with AEO status may place the goods under a procedure and then assumes the importer's obligations (recital 65), including compliance with non-financial requirements (recital 21).
- **When the debt arises.** Where the IOSS is used, the customs debt is incurred at the time the payment is accepted; otherwise, when the availability of the goods is notified in the Member State of delivery (recital 77).
- **Data.** IOSS users provide sales data at the latest on the day after the payment is accepted (recital 53).
- **Union handling fee.** The debtor is the importer for distance sales, never the consumer (recital 80). It has been €2 per item since 1 November 2026.
- **Customs warehouse for distance sales** (Article 145): only importers for distance sales that have chosen the IOSS and hold Trust and Check trader status, or their indirect representatives holding that status, can be authorised. Goods must already comply with the applicable product rules when they enter the warehouse, and are released for free circulation at the time of sale. Applicable from 1 July 2028 (Article 287(5)(a)).
- **Penalties** (Article 276): pecuniary penalties based on the value of goods imported in case of systematic non-compliance, loss of trusted statuses and, ultimately, restrictions on the online interface (recitals 105 and 106).

In the meantime, the temporary €3 duty per item has applied since 1 July 2026 to consignments of up to €150 (Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382). Normal duties will replace it once the Data Hub is operational. For how the Data Hub will work, see [the EU Customs Data Hub explained]({{ site.baseurl }}/blog/2026/09/23/eu-customs-data-hub-explained/).

## The combination: one transaction, one debtor, one moment

The target architecture is symmetrical: same scope, same chargeable event, same value and, ideally, the same operator for VAT and duties. The Council text says so explicitly: the deletion of the VAT threshold should correspond with the full customs regime applying to all imports under the new code (recital 3b).

| Dimension | VAT (VAT Directive after the reform) | Customs (Regulation (EU) 2026/2108) |
|---|---|---|
| Debtor | Deemed supplier (platform, Article 14a) or seller using the IOSS | Importer for distance sales: seller or facilitator (Article 5, point 14) |
| Scope | Distance sales of imported goods (Article 14(4), point 2), no threshold | Same definition (Article 5, point 15), no threshold |
| Chargeable event | Acceptance of payment (Article 66a) | Acceptance of payment where the IOSS is used (recital 77) |
| Value | B2C sale price | Transaction value based on the distance sale (recital 74) |
| Non-EU operator | IOSS intermediary, minimum criteria to come | Indirect representative with AEO status (recital 65) |
| Stock in the EU | IOSS extended to customs warehouses for distance sales only | Customs warehouse for distance sales (Article 145), reserved for IOSS users holding Trust and Check status |
| Exclusions | Excise goods | No equivalent exclusion identified |

For a non-EU seller, this points to a simple target model: one EU-established entity, acting both as IOSS intermediary and as indirect customs representative with AEO status, collects VAT and duties at checkout and answers to both administrations. It is a DDP model that is finally legally coherent, but also an unprecedented concentration of risk on that intermediary.

The constraint is tighter still for anyone who wants to hold stock in the EU. The customs warehouse for distance sales requires the importer, or its indirect representative, to hold Trust and Check status. That status requires, among other things, at least two years of regular customs operations and near-real-time access for customs to the operator's systems. Representatives holding it will be scarce at first.

## The blind spots: a "dual status" that is not always automatic

Talking of a single "deemed importer" mirroring the deemed supplier is appealing but partly inaccurate. Six friction points remain.

1. **A legal fiction on one side, a designation on the other.** For VAT, a facilitating platform is the deemed supplier by operation of law (Article 14a). For customs, the importer is the seller or the facilitator "depending on the arrangements made" (recital 63). A platform can therefore owe the VAT while the seller is declared as importer: two debtors for the same parcel.
2. **The IOSS remains optional.** Without the IOSS, import VAT is due from the person designated by the Member State of importation (Article 201 of the VAT Directive). That national debtor is not necessarily the importer for distance sales.
3. **Excise goods break the symmetry.** They stay outside the IOSS, yet their distance sales fall within the new customs status.
4. **Customs importer is not product-law economic operator.** A platform acting as importer for distance sales is not, by that fact alone, the responsible economic operator under Article 16 of the GPSR or Article 4 of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020.
5. **A timetable that depends on IT.** The draft directive provides for transposition by 30 June 2028 and application from 1 July 2028. If the IOSS functionality of the Data Hub is not operational by 1 June 2028, application is postponed until the transitional solution ends (Article 2 of the draft, referring to Article 285 of the new code). The €3 duty runs until 1 July 2028 unless extended.
6. **Asymmetric legal certainty.** The customs status is settled; the VAT status without threshold still requires unanimity. If the VAT text stalled, consignments above €150 would have a defined customs importer but VAT collected under national import rules.

The real issue is therefore not whether a dual status exists, but the **contractual alignment** of the two: who, in each flow, carries VAT and duties, and who answers if the other defaults. ## What operators should prepare now

Nineteen months is short to rework contracts, data flows and prices. Five priorities:

1. **Map the flows.** For each channel (own website, marketplace, B2B2C), identify who will be the deemed supplier for VAT and who will be the importer for distance sales for customs.
2. **Align the two statuses contractually.** Platform and seller terms should state who declares as importer, who bears duties and fees, and the cross-guarantees in case of reassessment.
3. **Choose the intermediary.** A non-EU seller will need an IOSS intermediary and an indirect representative with AEO status. Combining both roles in one entity simplifies the set-up, but concentrates financial and product-compliance liability.
4. **Rework DDP pricing.** After 2028, normal duty rates on all values, VAT at checkout and the handling fee: the displayed price must include everything.
5. **Prepare the data.** Tariff classification and origin per item, sales data sent no later than the day after payment and, if EU stock is planned in a customs warehouse for distance sales, an indirect representative holding Trust and Check status identified now: the IOSS is mandatory there and AEO status alone is not enough.
