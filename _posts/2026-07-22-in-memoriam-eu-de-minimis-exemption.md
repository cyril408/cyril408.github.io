<p><strong>In Memoriam &mdash; The EU De Minimis Exemption (&euro;0 &ndash; &euro;149.99)</strong><br>
Born: 1 July 2021 &middot; Departed: 1 July 2026 &middot; Age: five years of blissful naivety</p>

<p>It is with mixed emotions &mdash; and full customs declarations &mdash; that we bid farewell to an old friend: the EU de minimis threshold, which for half a decade let billions of small parcels slip through European borders duty-free, no questions asked.</p>

<p>You were generous. Perhaps too generous. Under your watch, a &euro;12 fast-fashion blouse from Guangzhou could reach a Brussels doorstep faster than a letter sent across town &mdash; no customs duty, a gentle nod to IOSS for the VAT, and on its way. You were the golden ticket behind a thousand Temu hauls.</p>

<p>The numbers, in retrospect, were staggering: roughly 4.6 billion small parcels entered the EU each year under your protection, a volume so large that customs authorities largely waved it through. European retailers watched, invoice in hand, while their Chinese competitors priced at landed cost and still turned a margin.</p>

<p>The IOSS system, your faithful companion, did its best &mdash; collecting VAT on low-value imports with real diligence. But customs duties remained your sacred shield. Until now.</p>

<h2>What changes on 1 July 2026</h2>

<p>All goods entering the EU are now subject to customs duties, regardless of value. The &euro;150 floor is gone. Every &euro;5 phone case, every &euro;8 lip gloss, every suspiciously well-priced pair of earbuds now faces the same tariff treatment as a container of industrial machinery. The playing field, at long last, levels.</p>

<p>For platforms like Shein, Temu, and AliExpress, the underlying economics change fundamentally. Their pricing architecture was built around this exemption; logistics hubs in Belgium and the Netherlands, optimised to exploit it at scale, now need recalibrating.</p>

<h2>A footnote from France</h2>

<p>France had already prepared its own farewell gift: a &euro;2 flat fee on small non-EU parcels, meant to offset the administrative burden the exemption created. Poetically, now that the EU-wide reform is in force, that measure is suspended &mdash; not because it was wrong, but because the broader regime makes it redundant. The &euro;2 fee steps aside so the customs duty can take over.</p>

<p>It will be mourned by cross-border logistics operators, dropshippers running thin margins, and anyone who ever ordered eight items &ldquo;to try&rdquo; and kept two. It will not be mourned by European SMEs, customs authorities, or anyone who ever had to explain to a client why a domestically-made product couldn't compete with a &euro;9 import.</p>

<p>Rest in paperwork.<br>
&mdash; The European Single Market, 1 July 2026</p>
